We had a dang good dove season here at 44 Bar Ranch. Every year we get some friends out for opening weekend. We hunt while there’s daylight, then pull our chairs up to the fire, crack something cold, and start tellin’ yarns. Funny how the birds get faster every time somebody explains why he missed. [Dove Camp 2026](/about/dove-camp-2026/)

## What Texas counted

I wanted to see how the rest of Texas did. The latest harvest numbers Texas Parks and Wildlife has posted are for **2025–26**. The department figures hunters took about **6.16 million mourning doves** and **1.86 million white-winged doves** across the state that season. As of November 13, I haven’t seen a statewide harvest tally for this fall. There’s still a winter split to hunt. [Texas Parks and Wildlife harvest survey](https://tpwd.texas.gov/huntwild/hunt/planning/harvest_surveys/)

There’s a fresh bird count, though. This spring, biologists estimated **36.7 million mourning doves** in Texas and **12.9 million white-wings**, the highest white-wing estimate they’ve recorded. That’s how many birds they figure were out there in the spring. How many came home in a hunter’s vest is a whole other count. [Texas Parks and Wildlife dove population report](https://tpwd.texas.gov/huntwild/wild/game_management/dove_summary/doveStatusReport.phtml)

## There’s still some season left

Regular dove hunting picks back up **December 11 in the Central Zone** and **December 18 in the North and South zones**. The closing dates vary, so look up your county before you load the truck. [Texas Parks and Wildlife dove dates and county regulations](https://tpwd.texas.gov/regulations/outdoor-annual/regs/animals/dove/)

When the state puts out this season’s harvest numbers, I’ll be curious to see how everybody else did. Around here, we’ve already got enough material to get us through another campfire. And if a fella tells you the same story twice, go easy on him. He might still be workin’ on the ending.
